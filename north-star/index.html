Somebody has a problem

That is where an idea starts, not with the idea. Put the person and what bothers them on a card each, in one plain sentence.

But is it true?

Everything starts as a guess. Interviews, numbers and things you saw count for it or against it, until you know.

Then comes your answer

This idea solves that problem, for them. The links hold the why, so it doesn’t live only in your head.

Together, they add up

A problem breaks into smaller ones, features ship as a package, and every piece stays tied to why it’s there.

Same thinking, as a list

The very same cards, now rows. Sort them, filter them, add a column for anything you track. Nothing is copied.

Find what nobody does

Set your features against your rivals’, cell by cell, with how sure you are. The row nobody fills is yours.

So you know what’s first

Impact, effort, and how much of it you have checked. North Star suggests an order. You decide.

And who does it by Friday

Tasks with a name and a day, a timeline, a conversation on any card. The work sits where the thinking is.

The whole idea, on one canvas

Every line of thought, every list and plan, side by side with your team in it. Zoom in to think, zoom out to see.

Look closer: it’s one card

On the plane, in the list, the matrix, the order, the tasks and the days. Not six copies. Change it once and it has changed everywhere.

With everything behind it

On top, one line. Underneath, the interviews, documents, arguments and tasks it rests on. Nobody reads all of that every time.

Your agent does.

Every card, link, document and conversation, down to the bottom. It sees what you don’t have on screen.

Ask it what matters

Bring an idea.
